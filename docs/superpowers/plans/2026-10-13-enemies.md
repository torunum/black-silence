# Enemies, redrawn: a sprite rig

The owner, 2026-10-08: "I want the character models developed further, in the
style." (And, earlier: more animation overall.) This plan is the enemy half of
that; the gore half is `2026-10-08-impact.md`.

- **Animation is two frames.** Walking swaps `a` and its mirror `b` every
  0.22 s (`Behaviors.ts` ~l.312); the attack is one frame (`atk`) held for
  0.22 s, only for the ten "redesigned" enemies; death is `die1`/`die2`, same
  ten; everything else collapses by rotating the sprite. No pain frame, no
  idle, no wind-up.
- **The bosses are the smallest, coarsest art in the game** — a 2.1 x 2.5 unit
  boss from a 16x13 grid — and they are what the player stares at longest.
- **Dismemberment is a rectangle mask** (`SpriteBaker.ts`): the same rows
  re-rendered with the head, arm or leg *region* blanked and wet stump pixels
  painted on the cut line (drawing from the seeded `Math.random` at boot). The
  head that flies off is a generic sprite, not the enemy's head.

## Approach

The same technique the owner accepted for the weapons: each enemy becomes a
**parametric rig** — boxes, prisms and ellipsoids with named parts (head,
torso, arms, legs, plus per-enemy extras: jaws, horns, wings, tails, flesh) —
posed by a function and **rasterized at boot** with the viewmodel's raster
(`src/render/viewmodel/{builder,raster}.ts`: hard tones, outline, rim light)
into per-pose billboard textures. The game keeps billboards; what changes is
how many frames exist, how big they are, and that the parts are real.

What a rig gives that hand-drawn grids cannot, at this scale of work:
- a real **walk cycle** (4-6 poses, legs and arms swinging), an idle,
  a three-phase attack (wind-up, strike, recover), pain and stagger frames,
  and a 4-5 frame death — for every enemy, not just ten;
- **real dismemberment**: the head-less, arm-less and leg-less variants omit
  the actual part and draw a stump (bone + meat) at the actual joint; the
  head, arm and leg that fly off are *that enemy's own parts*, baked as small
  sprites; a legless enemy gets a crawl cycle;
- one consistent look across all 22 reachable enemies, the weapons, and
  later the bosses at a size they deserve (96-128 px for bosses).

## Global Constraints

- `reference/sonsurum.html` is never edited. `src/enemies/pixels/*.ts` stays
  as the legacy data: `tests/fidelity.test.ts` pins it to the reference, and
  enemies not yet re-rigged keep using it. **Migrate incrementally**: an enemy
  with a rig uses it; one without uses its PXDEF exactly as today.
- No gameplay values change: HP, speed, damage, hit zones' *meaning* (a head
  is still the top ~26% — `Hitscan.ts` `frac>0.74` — define each rig's head
  region to match), sprite world size (`e.w/e.h`), attack timing.
- **Boot draws.** `buildSprites` draws `Math.random` at boot (stump colours),
  so it is part of the seeded stream every trace depends on. A rig bake must
  draw none; and to keep every fixture's gameplay identical, a
  `burnLegacySpriteDraws()` shim must consume *exactly the same number* of
  draws the legacy bake consumed for the enemies it replaced (derive the count
  from the legacy data; test it; delete the shim the day the fixtures are
  re-recorded anyway). Camera and HUD in all traces must stay identical.
- **Trace digests** name a sprite's texture by registry (`PX`...). Keep the
  rig textures registered in `PX` under the same role names (`a`, `b`, `atk`,
  `die1`, `die2`, `hl`, `noHead`...) and add new roles. A trace's scene digest
  will move where a walking or hurt enemy now selects a new frame — prove
  that is *all* that moved, field by field, per each header's procedure.
- Boot cost is measured and budgeted (target: all 22 enemies baked in under
  250 ms on this machine), no per-frame allocation, texture memory counted.
- The roster cut is pending the owner's call (25 -> 9 + 3 bosses); do not
  spend effort on the three unplaced enemies or invent new ones.
- No `src/` file over 400 lines; no import cycles; `npm test` green with the
  exit code captured; Write tool for scripts; never `git checkout --`;
  commit and push early; nothing untracked; **no `Co-Authored-By`**.

## Task 1 — the rig kit, the bestiary, and the ghoul

1. **The kit.** `src/enemies/rig/`: a part/joint model, a pose type
   (walk phase, attack phase, pain, death phase, dismember mask, spin of
   extras), a bake function `bakeEnemy(rig) -> frames`, and the shared
   palette/shading tuned to the weapons' look.
2. **The ghoul (`z`) end to end**: rig, walk (6), idle, attack (3), pain (2),
   death (5), dismembered variants with real stumps, its own flying parts.
3. **Integration**: `PX.z` gets the new frames; `Behaviors.ts` selects walk
   cycle / pain / attack phases / death sequence for any enemy that has them
   (legacy enemies unchanged); `Damage.ts`/`Death.ts` use the enemy's own
   parts for the head and limbs that fly; `Hitscan.ts` head test matches.
4. **The bestiary**: a page like the sound board (`/bestiary.html`, built
   into the published site, never imported by the game) listing every enemy
   with its frames animated, **old and new side by side** for each re-rigged
   enemy, with buttons to play walk / attack / pain / death / each
   dismemberment. The owner judges by eye on it.
5. Look at it in the game: frame strips of a ghoul walking at range and up
   close, attacking, hurt, dying, losing a head and a leg.

Commit: `feat: the ghoul is a rig — a real walk, a real death, real parts`
